01 — Framing

Primitive.

RTT LATENCY MEASUREMENT
  • A signal cannot travel faster than light — about 300 km per ms in vacuum, about 200 in fiber
  • Real routes are not great circles, so the bound is looser still
  • The node originates the challenge and times it on its own clock
  • The accelerator signs the nonce in confidential mode
  • The round trip gives an upper bound on distance, nothing more
  • A round trip can be delayed. It cannot be shortened
  • Brass and Aarne at IAPS laid out the policy case in 2024 — established technique
  • Over the internet the bound is hundreds of km: continents, not sites
  • Greg's 100 km is the starting hypothesis; the prototype calibrates it
  • Ask — anyone want to push on this before I build on it?

02 — Pipeline

Pipeline.

Collect evidence

Evidence log

Every challenge and response, signed and timed.

→
Assess evidence

Credibility assessment

Where the device was, with what confidence and qualifiers.

→
Evaluate policy

Determination

One answer per policy question.

  • Three jobs usually blended together. We separate them
  • Collect: challenges, responses, records — including the ones that never came back
  • Assess: the evidence function. Location evidence against the claim, producing a credibility assessment plus qualifiers
  • Formally: a posterior over space and time, conditional on the evidence
  • The middle stage is neutral — it neither measures nor decides. A relying party can re-run it
  • Evaluate: test that assessment against a policy. Change detection, containment, whatever the authority sets
  • "Evaluate" is reserved for policy; evidence is assessed

03 — Primitive

Lifecycle of a ping.

In a co-located node, almost none of this is distance.

  • Walk it left to right. This is the general shape, not Design 01 specifically
  • Landmark is the general term — in Design 01 the assurance node plays that role
  • The clock starts when the message leaves and stops when the signed message returns
  • Everything between is the measured interval. Verification and storage sit outside it
  • Two hops shown: the wide network and the facility LAN. Co-located collapses the first
  • The interval is a budget made of these elements — switching, queueing, driver, signing, and a little distance
  • Others on the team have done a fair bit of empirical work quantifying this. More to be done
  • This points at a class of attack: deflate the non-distance contributions so the device looks closer than it is
  • That buys room to move the device — provided you also subvert the continuity mechanisms
  • Ask — you run this hardware. Where could you add or hide delay, and which of these numbers do you already know?

04 — Design 01

Proposed architecture.

  • Builds in three: the attesters, then the node we install, then who the evidence is for
  • Attester: each covered accelerator with its host, in confidential-computing mode
  • The client on the host is trusted for nothing
  • Assurance node: our hardware in the rack, in an enclosure. It plays the landmark role
  • Devin and Andrew cover the enclosure
  • Assurance service: off site, serves many nodes, presents results to relying parties
  • Three step-downs below this slide: enrollment, evidence log, structured transparency. Only if asked
04 — Design 01

Enrollment.

  1. Install the node on site, with the operator present.
  2. Inspect the accelerators and record their identities.
  3. Run challenges long enough to characterize normal timing.
  4. Re-enroll after maintenance or updates, authorized by the verifier.
  • Characterize the installation at the start. Detect change after
  • Step three is the baseline — normal timing per accelerator
  • Proposed, not open for debate today: the challenge seed is loaded securely on the node at enrollment
04 — Design 01

Location evidence log.

04 — Design 01

Structured transparency.

Assess on siteOur leaning

Evidence stays on the node. Only policy outputs and qualifiers leave.

Assess off siteAlternative

Evidence leaves. Simpler, and a relying party can re-run the assessment.

Inbound either way: policies and approved versions, signed by the verifier.

  • What crosses the facility boundary is genuinely open
  • Our leaning: the node assesses evidence and evaluates policy locally
  • Alternative is simpler and more checkable by a relying party — that is a real argument
  • A node that stops reporting is a finding in itself
  • Ask — as breakers, which arrangement would you rather attack?
04 — Design 01

Assumptions*

  • Enrollment was secure
  • The device key cannot be copied
  • The enclosure is intact, or sensors say otherwise
  • Node software is as installed
  • Signatures are sound

* A proposed and incomplete list

  • Proposed, not settled — the question for the room is which of these should stay assumptions and which should be attackable
  • Deliberately not assumed: the node's clock is honest
  • Deliberately not assumed: the network is benign — the adversary controls it
  • We rely only on the fact that a round trip cannot be shortened
  • How the clock drifts and how it could be influenced is a prototype question
