Ballpark working threat scenario

A well-resourced organized criminal network seeks to operate 100+ declared H100 GPUs more than 500 km from their authorized site for over one month, while continuing to produce location evidence accepted by the verifier, with an attack budget of approximately $1M.
Assumptions to settle
  • Operator cooperation and physical/network access.
  • Whether deception begins at enrollment or after verified installation.
  • What the attack budget includes.
Required detection performance

Detect within [time], with [probability], at [acceptable false-alarm rate].

  • A ballpark to evaluate against, not a settled requirement
  • Attacker, scale, distance, duration and budget in one sentence
  • Ask: is this the right order of magnitude?
  • Is the operator cooperating, and what physical and network access do they have?
  • Enrollment deception means a declared GPU could be remote from the outset; no diversion event needed
  • Hardware, bribes, insiders, infrastructure?
  • Fill these in together; they set the numbers for every investigation that follows

Can we bind fresh responses to individual GPUs?

  • Essential premiseValid responses require a realtime connection to the target GPU.
  • Candidate approaches exist; suitability remains unestablished.
  • InvestigationTest device binding, freshness, and latency.
  • Priority1. Current confidence: moderately high.
  • The target GPU must respond to fresh challenges during the timed interval
  • If valid responses can be returned without a realtime connection to the GPU, the whole design is invalidated
  • Candidates: CC-mode signing, GPU fingerprinting (Tee and Happel), Hancke and Kuhn distance bounding, rounding-error analysis (Cankaya)
  • Not aware of experiments sufficient to establish any of them as suitable for secure LV
  • Spike: review and experimentally validate candidate primitives for RTT protocols
  • Freshness and device-binding tests
  • Hypothesis: a secure, low-latency primitive exists, assuming hardware vendor security; likely relies on CC mode
  • Importance extremely high, so first despite moderately high confidence

How much artificial delay can an attacker conceal?

  • Optimizing RTT does not by itself prevent inflated reference measurements.
  • Trusted reference measurements might expose deviations.
  • InvestigationDistinguish ordinary variation from artificial delay.Experiment: establish a fast path into a data center through an operator-controlled firewall; measure minimum achievable latency from a nearby (<10 km) landmark node.
  • DiscussionWould the remaining concealed delay enable the specified diversion?
  • Priority2. Very important; premise and test need sharpening.
  • An optimized RTT protocol leaves little latency budget for an attacker to deflate
  • But even optimized, I don't think we can stop an attacker inflating reference measurements
  • We may be able to detect it, or at least flag unusual behavior, against references from a trusted interconnection
  • Suspicion: loosely true but not to a useful degree; egregious deviations get caught, considerable added latency still looks normal
  • Can we set a limit that accepts ordinary operation and rejects inflated measurements? To what accuracy?
  • The firewall fast-path experiment gives a floor for achievable latency
  • How much delay can hide under the limit, and does that enable the scenario we agreed?
  • Still too woolly to rank higher; what is the actual premise?
  • Ask: does this solve the enrollment problem?

Can we establish that declared GPUs are physically present?

  • Without initial verification, GPUs could be remote from the outset.
  • Physical identifiers must be reliably associated with cryptographic identities.
  • InvestigationAccess feasibility, identity checks, sampling requirements.
  • DiscussionDo we need “all declared are present,” “all present are declared,” or both?
  • Priority3.
  • No diversion event needed if a GPU was never on site
  • With a verified inventory, initial measurements are of real, present GPUs; with enough sampling, disconnections become easier to detect
  • Key uncertainty: physical-to-digital device association
  • Interview a production data center operations expert: can a verifier with physical access check GPU identities? Time cost, access needed
  • Model the number of samples needed to reach threshold confidence
  • Intuition: “all declared are present” is very valuable for location via the continuity argument
  • “All present are declared” matters less for the location claim, more for other claims
  • Hypothesis: with physical access, an inventory procedure can detect at least X missing or undeclared GPUs with 99% probability
  • Likelihood fairly high; importance moderate to high

Can we check frequently enough at acceptable cost?

  • Frequent checks may expose disconnection during diversion.
  • Useful frequency depends on the threat model.
  • InvestigationBenchmark challenges under GPU load.
  • HypothesisSufficient frequency is possible at <1% performance cost.
  • Priority3.
  • Some diversions need the chip disconnected for a transport period
  • Detecting disconnection, plus an AN that is hard to move, makes transport considerably harder
  • “Enough” is defined by the detection timeframe in the threat model
  • Measure the performance impact of identification primitives while the GPU is under load
  • Counterweight: if useful frequencies degrade performance, operators will strongly resist the loss of capital efficiency
  • Likelihood fairly high

Critical dependency:
can we trust and locate the Assurance Node?

  • Challenges, timing, and evidence handling must remain secure.
  • Moving the AN and GPUs together must not preserve accepted evidence.
  • RequiredA named owner and convincing validation.
  • DiscussionWhat exactly must be demonstrated?
  • PriorityVery high, but currently outside scope.
  • If this is not true, the design does not work
  • The AN must issue unpredictable challenges, measure time, and store, process and serve evidence
  • What prevents an operator moving the node and GPUs together while still producing accepted evidence?
  • Needs an owner and evidence-based validation before I believe this dependency will be met
  • “Design a secure AN and red team it” is not an investigation; what is the crux premise?
  • We assumed this was out of scope; we should revisit that

Would the deployment requirements be workable?

  • AN placement affects access, security, and operator acceptance.
  • Performance cost, disruption, and disclosure may constrain deployment.
  • InvestigationOperator interviews and comparable verification mandates.
  • DiscussionWhich deployment context are we designing for, and what counts as feasible enough?
  • A policy question, but system design can inform policy design
  • An AN outside the data center may provoke less resistance than one in a sensitive interior
  • Industry may lobby hard against mandates for ANs inside firewalls; external landmarks may be more palatable and easier to secure
  • Industry groups will lobby to minimize lost capital efficiency
  • Interview DC operators and AI providers
  • Find comparable mandates and the industry response; some may impose <2% costs
  • Political will depends on variables that move the Overton window
  • Hard to judge without a projected upside; what measurable experiments, if any, can we run?
