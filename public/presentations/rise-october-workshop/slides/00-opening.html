Threat model

Who can do what?

Verifier access regime

What may the verifier inspect or operate?

  • Threat model: the adversaries, capabilities and assumed protections under consideration
  • Verifier access regime: the physical, technical and institutional access available to the verifier, e.g. inspection, installation, measurement, updates, evidence access
  • They interact: a check may depend on access that is unavailable; a protection may depend on excluding a capability the adversary actually has
  • Also examine the operator's exposure to a malicious or compromised verifier

Problem statement: threat model

Compute verification has a different threat model than cybersecurity

SecurityVerification
One adversary (outside the fortress)Two adversaries: verifier outside; prover inside
Secret needs to stay in the fortressSecret needs to stay in the fortress + the verifier needs correct, limited information about the secret
Defender loses if the attacker captures the flag once

Prover loses if the verifier captures the flag

Prover loses if the verifier catches cheating (once is enough)

Verifier loses if the prover cheats on a relevant scale
  • Security: one adversary, outside the fortress
  • Verification: two adversaries; the verifier is outside, the prover inside
  • The secret must stay in, and the verifier still needs correct, limited information about it
  • Asymmetric losses: the prover loses on one caught cheat; the verifier loses only if cheating happens on a relevant scale
